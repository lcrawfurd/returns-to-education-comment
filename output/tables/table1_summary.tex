Panel~B applies selection / significance-based models to study-collapsed estimates ($n=48$): p-uniform* \citep{vanAert2021}, Copas \citep{Copas2001}, Andrews--Kasy \citep{AndrewsKasy2019}, and multiple-bias meta-analysis \citep{Mathur2024} at the selection intensity implied by Andrews--Kasy ($\eta \approx 2$) (R packages \texttt{puniform}, \texttt{metasens}, \texttt{weightr}, \texttt{multibiasmeta}). p-uniform* CI not available (bootstrap non-convergence with extreme heterogeneity). Andrews--Kasy assumes 5\%-significance-based selection; $\hat{\omega} = 0.50$ indicates moderate selection. Worst-case bounds (which assume the within-study $p$-hacking the caliper test does not detect): right-truncated meta-analysis (RTMA, \texttt{phacking}) gives a corrected mean near 2--4\% (credible interval excludes zero), and multiple-bias under extreme selection ($\eta=200$) gives 1.4\%. Panel~C reproduces CN's own estimates: PET-PEESE on their replication do-file sample ($\mathrm{SE}<10.1\%$), OLS extrapolation, and normal-distribution fit. Samples differ by method as each method's assumptions require (column~2); no estimate is dropped as an outlier from any row, the only outlier exclusions in the paper being presentational, in the funnel and histogram figures. $^{\dag}$ Egger intercept is a dimensionless $t$-statistic (expected $t$-value as precision $\to 0$), not a bias-corrected return in percentage points. $^{\ddag}$ PET is the precision-effect significance test; it rejects a zero effect in every specification here ($p<0.001$), so under the PET--PEESE decision rule the reported bias-corrected estimate is the PEESE intercept (6.1\%, 5.0\%, and 5.4\% under FE, RE, and RVE weighting). The PET intercept is shown for completeness, not as a separate estimate. `---' denotes no analytic $p$-value.
